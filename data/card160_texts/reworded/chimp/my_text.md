Engelmann et al. present evidence that chimpanzees also prepare for multiple possible outcomes, and propose that they too represent exclusive-OR relations. [[chimp]]

Engelmann et al.’s study used an adaptation of our forked tube task , in which an experimenter drops a reward into an inverted Y-shaped tube with two exits, so that a participant has to cover both exits at the same time, one hand on each, to be sure of catching it (also ). [[chimp]]

In our studies, we have found little evidence that non-human primates prepare insightfully for mutually exclusive possibilities in this simple context . [[chimp]]

In our experiments with 14 primates (five chimpanzees across two studies , five orangutans in one study , and four monkeys in one study ), we found that three chimpanzees, one orangutan and one capuchin monkey covered both exits spontaneously although they had never seen that behaviour before. [[chimp]]

On subsequent trials, each of these five individuals regressed to covering one exit. [[chimp]]

When two rewards were simultaneously dropped into two parallel tubes, chimpanzees covered two exits spontaneously on 24.4% of trials, a rate seven times higher than when only one reward was unpredictably dropped into one of the two mutually exclusive tubes (on 3.4% of trials; see table 1 in ). [[chimp]]

The three individuals who learned to cover both exits consistently near the end of the simultaneous trials (i.e. passing 90–100% of the final 10 trials) went on to pass only 17–58% of mutually exclusive trials in a supplementary experiment. [[chimp]]

In the mutually exclusive trials the apes had to cover both exits to guarantee a reward, but in the simultaneous trials they could simply cover one exit to guarantee a reward (see box 1 in ). [[chimp]]

According to the data, the primates’ difficulty in preparing for mutually exclusive possibilities was at least partly in their minds and not simply in their hands. [[chimp]]

Three individuals had to be excluded [from Engelmann et al.'s study], as they found the target behaviour too difficult (see supplementary material ). [[chimp]]

According to the preregistration, the remaining chimpanzees received up to three attempts per trial if they failed to balance either platform, and trials were excluded if they did not. [[chimp]]

In the control condition 7/16 chimpanzees balanced both platforms on their first trial, and in the test condition 8/16 did so. [[chimp]]
